
# Home Learning Plan for [Son's Name] — Age 7, Starting Primary Two
Built for Mimi, Bamenda, Cameroon — 4 weeks before school starts (school begins October due to ghost town period)

## PHILOSOPHY
He learns through EXCITEMENT and MOTIVATION, not fear. He already proved this by teaching himself video games in a day.
Everything below is designed as: Learn first (small bite) -> Earn screen time (reward) -> Repeat.
No shouting, no beating. Consistency and small wins matter far more than long sessions.
Sessions should be 10 to 15 minutes MAX per subject. He has a short attention span right now — that is normal, not failure.

---

## DAILY ROUTINE (Monday to Saturday, adjust Sunday for church)

Morning:
1. Wake up
2. Pray together
3. Brush teeth, wash face
4. Eat breakfast
5. LEARNING BLOCK (see below) — before any screen time
6. Screen time / play (earned, time-boxed, e.g. 45 minutes to 1 hour)
7. Free play with toys

Afternoon (optional second block):
8. Short review of morning lesson (5 minutes, playful, no pressure)
9. Screen time earned again if he engages

Evening:
10. Wind-down, talk about his day, recite the poem or prayer of the week
11. Bed

Use a simple visual chart on the wall: draw 3 boxes per day (Reading, Math, Talking/Social). He puts a sticker or draws a star in the box himself when done. Seeing progress matters more to a child like him than being told he did well.

---

## SUBJECT 1: READING (the main target — starting from absolute zero)

Goal over 4 weeks: Recognize all 26 letters by sound, blend simple 3-letter words (cat, dog, sun), and write his name and a few words without reversing letters.

Week 1 — Letter sounds, not letter names
- Day 1 to 2: Teach A, B, C, D, E — say the SOUND not the name ("ah" not "ay"). Use objects around the house: A is for Ant, B is for Ball. Let him touch the object.
- Day 3 to 4: F, G, H, I, J — same method. Trace the letter in sand, rice, or with his finger in the air (this helps with the reversal problem — his hand and eyes learn direction together).
- Day 5 to 6: Review all 10 letters through a game: you say the sound, he runs to touch an object in the house that starts with it.
- Day 7: Fun review day — sing an alphabet song together, no pressure, just fun.

Week 2 — More letters and first blending
- Day 1 to 3: K, L, M, N, O, P — same sound method.
- Day 4 to 5: Introduce blending with 3 letters he knows: "c-a-t" — stretch the sounds slowly, then say it fast. Use word cards with a picture.
- Day 6 to 7: Practice writing the letters learned so far — big, on paper with lines if possible. If he reverses a letter, do NOT correct harshly — just say "let's trace it together" and guide his hand.

Week 3 — Remaining letters and simple words
- Day 1 to 3: Q, R, S, T, U, V — sound method.
- Day 4 to 5: W, X, Y, Z — finish the alphabet.
- Day 6 to 7: Build 5 simple 3-letter words: cat, dog, sun, pen, cup. He reads them, then draws a picture of each.

Week 4 — Consolidation
- Day 1 to 3: Review all letters and words learned. Play "I spy" using letter sounds.
- Day 4 to 5: Introduce his name spelling and 2 to 3 sight words (his name, "mama", "yes", "no").
- Day 6 to 7: Small celebration — read a very simple picture book together, let him "read" the pictures and any words he knows.

Daily reading session structure (10 minutes):
1. Review yesterday's letter/word (1 minute)
2. New letter or word with object/picture (5 minutes)
3. Trace or write it (3 minutes)
4. Quick praise and sticker on chart (1 minute)

## SUBJECT 2: MATH (he already counts on fingers — build from there)

Goal: Count to 20 confidently, recognize written numbers 1 to 10, simple addition and subtraction with objects.

Week 1: Numbers 1 to 10 — say them, recognize the written digit, match to objects (count stones, spoons, toys).
Week 2: Numbers 11 to 20, and writing digits 1 to 10 without reversing them (same tracing method as letters).
Week 3: Simple addition using real objects — "you have 2 stones, I give you 1 more, how many now?" Keep it physical, not abstract.
Week 4: Simple subtraction the same way, plus a review game mixing addition and subtraction with a reward if he gets it right.

Daily math session structure (10 minutes):
1. Count objects together (2 minutes)
2. New concept with objects, not just numbers on paper (5 minutes)
3. One or two practice problems (2 minutes)
4. Sticker on chart (1 minute)

## SUBJECT 3: TALKING AND SOCIAL LIFE (communication and everyday interaction)

Goal: Move from repeating TV phrases to constructing his own sentences and answering personal questions about himself.

This is less about a "lesson" and more about daily conversation habits. Do these throughout the day, not just in one block.

Narrate your world out loud constantly. When you cook, say what you're doing: "I am cutting the tomato now, it is red." This models real sentence structure for him to absorb, the same way TV language got absorbed, but from you instead.

Ask open questions, not yes-or-no ones. Instead of "did you play well," ask "what did you play today, tell me one thing." Give him time to search for words, do not jump in and finish his sentence for him.

Weekly conversation topics to work through, a little each day:
Week 1: Basic personal facts — his name, his age, his favorite color, his favorite food. Ask each question, if he cannot answer, tell him the answer, then ask again a few minutes later.
Week 2: Simple choices and preferences — "do you like rice or plantain better, why." Push gently for "why," even a one-word reason is progress.
Week 3: Instructions and following directions — practice "please bring me the cup from the table" style requests, starting with only one item in view, then slowly adding more items nearby to build his search-and-select skill.
Week 4: Greetings and social interaction — practice how to greet someone, how to say his name when asked, how to ask a question back. Role-play meeting a new person, take turns being the visitor and the host.

A simple daily habit: every evening, ask him to tell you one thing about his day in his own words. If he only repeats a TV phrase, gently ask "but what happened to YOU today," and wait.

## RHYTHM AND ROUTINE ELEMENTS (things to recite together)

Pick one short prayer, poem, or rhyme per week and recite it together every morning after prayer. Repetition of the same short piece daily builds memory and confidence without new pressure. Example: a short two-line prayer, or a simple nursery rhyme like "twinkle twinkle little star." Let him say it with you, even if he mumbles half of it at first.

---

## SCREEN TIME STRUCTURE

Learning block first, always, before any screen access.
After learning block: 45 minutes to 1 hour of earned screen time.
If he refuses to do the learning block, hold firm and calm: no screen until the small task is done, but keep the task genuinely small so this is winnable, not a battle. Ten minutes of real focus is a win, do not push for more in the early weeks.

---

## A NOTE FOR MIMI

You are not starting late, you are starting right. He needs excitement based learning because of how his brain works, not more pressure. Four weeks of gentle, consistent, tiny steps before school starts is more valuable than trying to catch him up all at once. Track his wins on the wall chart, not the days he struggles. Progress here will look slow up close but real over these four weeks.
